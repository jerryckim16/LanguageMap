    Sound correspondences

    Words written with the same characters often sound alike, because Sino-Korean and Sino-Japanese readings both come from Middle Chinese, and syllable endings match up in regular ways. Characters are grouped below by the final consonant (받침) of their Korean reading. Click a character to see it in the network.

    About this map

    This map shows how the vocabulary of Korean lines up with Japanese. Each Korean word points to the Japanese word or words used for the same meaning.

    Bijections, splits and merges

    • One-to-one (↔): 달 ↔ 月. Both words mean "moon" and "month", so you can translate back and forth.
    • Split (1 → 2): Korean 머리 is both 頭 (head) and 髪 (hair) in Japanese.
    • Merge (2 → 1): Korean 다리 (leg) and 발 (foot) are both 足 in Japanese.
    • Homonyms are separate words with a small number: 눈¹ (eye) and 눈² (snow).
