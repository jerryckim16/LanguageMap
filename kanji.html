    Sound correspondences

    Sino-Korean and Sino-Japanese readings both come from Middle Chinese, so syllable endings match up in regular ways. Characters are grouped below by the final consonant (받침) of their Korean reading. Click a character to see it on the map.

    About this map

    About 60% of Korean vocabulary and about half of Japanese vocabulary are made from Chinese characters: hanja (한자) in Korean and kanji (漢字) in Japanese. Many words are built from the same characters in both languages, so learning one language gives you a head start on the other.

    How to read the map

    • Rounded boxes are word pairs: the Korean word on top, the Japanese word below.
    • Circles are the characters the words are made of. Big circles appear in many words.
    • A small character at the top right of a circle is the traditional form Korean uses where Japanese uses a simplified one (學 → 学).
    • Dashed red borders mark false friends: words written the same way that mean different things.

    Notes

    • Korean is romanized with Revised Romanization, Japanese with Hepburn.
    • Pronunciation (🔊) uses your browser's built-in speech voices, which may not exist on every device.
    • The word list is hand-curated. To add words, edit data/words.js.
    • For translations between any words, including ones with no shared characters, see the Dictionary map.
